# Describing Kenyans as real, varied people

Image models learned "African" mostly from stock photos, aid campaigns and travel imagery. Generic words pull those patterns back in. Describe each person the way you would brief a casting director: as an individual from a real place.

## 1. Anchor every person to a real place

Give the town, estate or area, and an age, every time.
- Weak: "a Kenyan man", "a black woman", "African people"
- Strong: "a man in his 30s from Kisumu", "a woman in her 50s who runs a shop in Nyeri", "a teenager from Kibera on his way to school", "a young professional living in Kilimani", "a fisherman in his 60s from Lamu"

Name a community (Kikuyu, Luo, Luhya, Kalenjin, Kamba, Kisii, Meru, Mijikenda, Maasai, Samburu, Turkana, Somali, Swahili, Kenyan Indian and others) only when it matters to the scene, such as a cultural ceremony or a traditional wedding. Never use it as a shortcut for "how they look": people in every community vary widely.

## 2. Describe skin tone in plain words

Do not rely on "black" or "dark-skinned" alone. Pick a depth and an undertone:
- **Depth:** light brown, golden brown, caramel, medium brown, warm brown, chestnut, deep brown, very deep brown, rich dark brown
- **Undertone:** warm (golden, red or copper), neutral, cool (blue or plum)
- Examples: "medium brown skin with warm golden undertones", "very deep brown skin with cool undertones", "light caramel skin with a few freckles on the nose"

In a group, give different people different tones. Real Kenyan families, classrooms and offices vary a lot.

## 3. Describe hair specifically

- **Texture:** tightly coiled (4C), coily (4A-4B), curly, wavy, straight
- **Natural styles:** short cropped, faded with a sharp line-up, a clean shave, teeny afro, full afro, twist-out, finger coils, high puff, bantu knots
- **Protective and styled:** box braids (short or long), knotless braids, cornrows, Fulani braids, passion twists, locs (short, mid-length, long), faux locs, African threading
- **Straightened and added:** relaxed bob, silk press, wigs (straight bob, curly, coloured), weaves
- **Covered:** dhuku or headscarf, hijab, kofia, cap, beanie on a cold Nairobi morning
- Vary hair across a set. Not every woman has braids and not every man has a fade.

## 4. Describe faces and bodies as individuals

Pick one or two specific details per person instead of "beautiful" or "handsome":
- face: round, oval, long, square jaw, high cheekbones, full cheeks, broad forehead, dimples, a gap in the front teeth, laugh lines, a small scar, freckles
- nose and lips: broad, narrow, straight, full, thin (variety is real)
- eyes: deep-set, wide-set, hooded, almond-shaped, glasses
- body: slim, average, stocky, tall and lanky, short and broad, plus-size, pregnant, athletic, older and slightly stooped
- age: give an age or decade every time, and include children, teenagers, older adults and elders, not only people in their 20s

Add "ordinary looks, not a model, not airbrushed" when the model drifts toward glossy faces.

## 5. Clothing that fits the place and the moment

- **Everyday:** office shirts with rolled sleeves, chinos, jeans, t-shirts, hoodies, sweaters on cold Nairobi mornings, sneakers, dresses, long skirts, school uniforms with the sweater tied round the waist, work overalls, reflective vests, gumboots on a farm.
- **Prints, used correctly:** kitenge shirts and dresses (often on Fridays or at events), kanga and leso wraps at home or at the coast, ankara at weddings. Not on everyone.
- **Occasion and traditional dress, only when the occasion calls for it:** kanzu with a coat and kofia (coast, Muslim events, some weddings), buibui (coast), Maasai shuka and beadwork at Maasai cultural events, ruracio and traditional wedding outfits, church choir robes, graduation gowns.
- **Small details that read as Kenyan:** a Kenyan flag beaded bracelet, a lanyard, a smartwatch, an umbrella in rainy season, a reflective vest on a boda rider.

## 6. Skin rendering line (use in every photo with people)

Correct exposure for every skin tone in the frame, with natural undertones and real texture: visible pores, a little natural sheen on the forehead or nose, no grey or ashy cast, no orange cast, no lightening, no plastic or oily shine, and no crushed shadows that hide facial features. Deep skin tones keep their detail and warmth.

For flat-light candid shots add: matte skin with natural texture, no glow.

## 7. Words to avoid, and what to use instead

| Avoid | Why | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| "black African", "Kenyan person", "African people" | collapses everyone into one stereotyped look | town plus individual description |
| "tribal", "tribe", "native", "exotic", "primitive" | pulls in costume, body paint and othering | the named community and occasion, or nothing |
| "village" as the default setting | pulls in mud huts and poverty | the actual town, estate or farm |
| "poor", "struggling", "in need", "rural Africa" | pulls in charity imagery | the real situation and action |
| "African print" on everyone | uniform, costume-like | specific clothing per person |
| "beautiful Kenyan woman" | produces the same glossy model face | an individual description and a real action |
| "dark-skinned" alone | over-darkens and loses detail | depth plus undertone, plus the skin rendering line |
| "safari", "savannah sunset", "acacia silhouette" | tourist shorthand | the actual place the story happens |
| "traditional" without a name | invents a fake costume | the named garment and occasion, or modern clothes |

## 8. Groups and crowds

- Describe 2-3 people in a group individually, then add: "the others vary in age, build, skin tone and hair, as you would expect in [place]".
- For a city crowd, name the place ("a lunchtime crowd on Kenyatta Avenue", "the Saturday crowd at Gikomba market") and say people are going about their own business.
- Include Kenyans of Somali, Swahili, Indian, Arab and mixed heritage, and white Kenyans, where they fit: Eastleigh, the coast, Parklands, Westlands, Karen, Mombasa Old Town.
