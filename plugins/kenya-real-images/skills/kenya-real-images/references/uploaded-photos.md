# Uploaded photos of a person

The user attached photos of a real person and wants them in a new scene. Generate ONE image straight away: no character sheet, no preview round. Think silently, then generate. The SKILL.md checklist still applies. If there is only one photo, mention once that front, profile and full-body photos help.

**Consent:** "me" or "my photo": go ahead. Someone else: ask once whether they agreed, unless already said. Never a celebrity or public figure. A child only when the user is clearly the parent or guardian, in ordinary family, school or everyday scenes.

## Identity block (write once, reuse word for word in later images)

- **Face:** shape and width, cheeks, forehead, eyes, brows, nose, lips, teeth, hairline, haircut, where facial hair grows.
- **Skin:** depth and undertone.
- **Body as measurements:** height in heads ("about 7 heads tall"), shoulders in head-widths, how chest, stomach, arms and legs compare to average, and what the body is NOT ("not heavy, not tall and slim"). In t-shirts, hoodies or sportswear, add "arms of an ordinary adult who does not lift weights, clothes hang loosely", or the body drifts heavier.
- **Accessories** and which wrist. Do not copy the photo's clothes unless asked.

## Stop the pasted face

- An expression that fits the moment and differs from the photos ("mid-sentence, eyebrows raised", "concentrating, lips closed").
- "Head, neck and shoulders turn together in one natural pose."
- "The face is lit by the same light as the rest of the scene."
- End with: "Use the attached photos ONLY for this person's identity (face, hairline, facial hair, skin tone and body proportions). Do NOT copy their pose, expression, head angle, smile, framing, lighting, background, clothing or any watermark."

## Make the scene logical

- A presenter has something on the screen: a simple chart or photo with no words, never blank. Gestures point at it; gaze agrees.
- At a screen: centre at eye level, an arm's length in front of the face, "eyes fixed on the middle of the screen"; camera from the side or behind-side, never in front of the screen.
- Presenting to people: they look at one named listener or the screen; listeners face them.
- Camera positions must be possible ("behind the shoulder" plus "face visible" becomes "beside and slightly behind at 45 degrees").
- Wide shots stay wide; make the person the sharpest, best-lit figure instead of cropping in. Otherwise keep the face at least a fifth of the frame height.
- Children's age by size: "an 8-year-old girl, her head reaching his chest".
- Drop hyper-realistic, cinematic, 8K and lens numbers unless a studio or ad look is clearly wanted.

## Order

Style opening, identity block, action and expression, layout (`space.md`), gaze, place, camera, one light, skin line, style block, format, reference rule. Reply in one line on what changed; if likeness or body looks off, offer one specific fix.
