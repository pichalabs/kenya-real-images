# Long, engineered and charity prompts

Prompts written by ChatGPT or prompt guides (8K, cinematic, lens specs, `--ar`, JSON, weights, negative prompts, ten people doing ten things) pull the image toward the glossy AI look. Keep the intent, drop the wording, and write a fresh prompt with the order in SKILL.md.

## Keep

Who (roles, ages, relationships), where, the one main action and the user's key verbs, the main subject, the purpose, the format, exact words that must appear, and a real style wish (illustration, studio headshot, named ad campaign).

## Drop or translate

- Quality words (8K, ultra-detailed, masterpiece, award-winning, trending, sharp focus), "hyper-realistic", "photorealistic": drop; the candid style gives realism.
- Cinematic, epic, stunning, magazine-worthy, polished, sleek, editorial, National Geographic, portrait: drop; they never choose the style.
- Flawless, perfect or glowing skin, retouching: "real skin texture".
- Camera bodies, lenses, f-stops, film stocks, bokeh, HDR, vivid, high contrast: drop.
- Golden hour: "late afternoon sun" only if the time matters.
- Neon, teal and orange, rim light: only on a wall or background, never on faces.
- Low angle, Dutch tilt, fisheye, drone of faces: level camera.
- `--ar 16:9`, `1080x1350`: the aspect ratio. Other flags, seeds, `(word:1.4)`, brackets: strip the syntax, keep real content words.
- Empty placeholders (`[STYLE]`): pick a default; never write brackets.
- Negative prompts: keep real wishes (no extra fingers, no watermark) as plain sentences; ignore bias words in them (poor, dirty, slum).
- "Everyone looking at the camera, confident smiles": only for a posed group photo or portrait; otherwise people look at each other or the task.
- Readable notes or boards without the words: soft, unreadable writing.
- Real landmarks (KICC, skyline): small in the background if they matter.
- Brand logos: keep only if the user named the brand.

Never write dropped words, not even as "no 8K".

## Cut to one believable photo

At most 2-3 main people with clear faces (none if the subject is not a person); one action; one light. Resolve contradictions by choosing what the user most likely meant (candid over polished, faces over drone view). A presenter looks at a listener or the screen; listeners look at the presenter or the screen.

## Charity, poverty and hardship

Never the "poor African child": sad eyes into the lens, dusty face, torn clothes, empty bowl, mud huts. Keep the real intent (a rural place, a real need, an NGO purpose) and show people with agency: walking to school with a sibling, carrying a jerrycan from a water point with a friend, studying by a solar lamp, queueing at a mobile clinic. Show the need through the setting (distance to water, dry field, crowded clinic), not faces or rags. Ordinary worn clothes, varied real expressions, nobody staring into the lens. Documentary style, a named place (a homestead near Lodwar, a village in Kitui). Tell the user in one line that you showed the person doing something real instead of the empty-bowl look.

## Tell the user

One plain line on what changed, e.g. "I kept your market scene with three people and dropped the 8K, lens and HDR settings so it looks like a real photo." No lecture. If the user says "use my prompt exactly" or "keep it cinematic", follow their style wording, but keep the skin line, bias, charity, gaze and layout rules.
